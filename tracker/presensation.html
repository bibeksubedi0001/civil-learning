1. Pollution (Global)

Pollution involves the introduction of harmful materials into the environment at a rate faster than it can be dispersed or degraded (National Geographic Society, 2024). It is a critical Transboundary Issue, meaning pollutants generated in one country often affect the health and ecosystem of neighboring nations (Somers, 1987; UNECE, 1979).

Air Pollution

  • Primary Pollutants: PM2.5, NO$_x$, SO$_2$, CO.
  • Indoor Pollution: Solid fuel burning.
  • Impact: 7 million premature deaths annually (WHO).

Water Pollution

  • Sources: Untreated industrial effluent, agricultural runoff, sewage.
  • Eutrophication: Nutrient excess leading to algal blooms and dead zones.
  • Microplastics: Found even in the deepest trenches.

Land/Soil Pollution

  • Toxins: Pesticides, heavy metals (Lead, Mercury), E-waste.
  • Consequence: Enters the food chain (Biomagnification).

Case Study: The Great Pacific Garbage Patch

1.6 Million km$^2$

The Great Pacific Garbage Patch, located between Hawaii and California, represents a massive accumulation of floating plastic that covers approximately 1.6 million square kilometers, an area roughly three times the size of France (Lebreton et al., 2018). This phenomenon highlights the durability of synthetic materials and the global scale of marine pollution, which is estimated to impact over 700 marine species through ingestion or entanglement (Gall & Thompson, 2015; NOAA, 2023).

2. Global Warming

The Greenhouse Effect Mechanism

Global warming refers to the unusually rapid increase in Earth’s average surface temperature over the past century primarily due to the greenhouse gases released as people burn fossil fuels (NASA Earth Observatory, 2010).

Key Greenhouse Gases (GHGs)

  • Carbon Dioxide (CO$_2$): Primary driver. 420+ ppm today vs 280 ppm pre-industrial.
  • Methane (CH$_4$): 25x more potent than CO$_2$. Sources: Livestock, rice paddies.
  • Nitrous Oxide (N$_2$O): 300x potency. Sources: Fertilizers, industrial processes.
The Albedo Effect Feedback Loop

As ice melts, its highly reflective white surface is replaced by the darker ocean, which significantly lowers surface albedo and increases the absorption of solar radiation. This process accelerates warming in a self-reinforcing mechanism known as the ice-albedo positive feedback loop (NASA, 2024; Serreze & Barry, 2011).

Global Temperature Anomaly (°C)

The planet has warmed by approx 1.1°C to 1.2°C since the late 19th century.

3. Overpopulation

Straining Earth's Carrying Capacity

The global human population officially surpassed the milestone of 8 billion in November 2022 (United Nations, 2022). Although the annual growth rate is decelerating, the sheer magnitude of the population continues to amplify environmental pressures, effectively acting as a multiplier for resource depletion and ecological strain (United Nations, 2022; UNEP, 2019).

Resource Intensity

It's not just numbers, but consumption patterns. A developed nation citizen consumes up to 30x more resources than someone in a developing nation.

Direct Consequences

  • Urbanization: Loss of arable land to concrete.
  • Waste Generation: Overwhelmed landfills.
  • Water Stress: High demand for agriculture and sanitation.
"The impact on the environment is the product of Population, Affluence, and Technology (I = PAT)."

Exponential Growth Trajectory

Demographic Shift

Most future growth will occur in Africa and South Asia, regions highly vulnerable to climate change impacts.

4. Resource Depletion

Running on Borrowed Time

Humanity is currently consuming resources at a rate equivalent to 1.7 Earths, meaning we are using nature's resources 1.7 times faster than the planet's ecosystems can regenerate them (Global Footprint Network, 2023). This "deficit spending" of ecological capital is unsustainable and leads to the depletion of fisheries, degradation of forests, and accumulation of carbon in the atmosphere (Lin et al., 2018; WWF, 2022).

Freshwater Crisis

Only 2.5% of water is fresh. Aquifers are draining faster than recharge rates. Example: The drying of the Aral Sea.

Rare Earth Minerals

Essential for electronics and batteries (Lithium, Cobalt). Mining causes severe ecological damage and geopolitical tension.

Sand Shortage

The world is running out of construction-grade sand, leading to illegal mining and riverbed destruction.

Years Left of Proven Reserves

*Estimates based on current consumption rates. New technologies may extend these, but the environmental cost of extraction rises.

5. Climate Change (Global)

Systemic Disruption

While global warming specifically refers to rising temperatures, Climate Change encompasses the broader range of resulting phenomena, such as extreme weather events, rising sea levels, and shifting wildlife populations (NASA, 2024). International security experts classify this as a "threat multiplier," meaning it does not just create new problems but exacerbates existing social, political, and economic instabilities (CNA Corporation, 2007; United Nations, 2009).

Social Impact: Climate Refugees

The UN estimates up to 200 million people could be displaced by 2050 due to rising sea levels, desertification, and resource conflict.

Manifestations:

  • Hydrological Cycle: Wet areas get wetter (floods), dry areas get drier (droughts).
  • Cryosphere: Glacial retreat threatening fresh water for millions in the Himalayas.
  • Ocean Circulation: Potential slowing of the AMOC current.

Sea Level Rise

Thermal expansion & melting ice. Threatens cities like Jakarta, NYC, Mumbai.

Heatwaves

"Wet-bulb" temperatures becoming unlivable in parts of the tropics.

Vector Disease

Mosquitoes migrating to higher latitudes/altitudes spread Malaria/Dengue.

Food Security

Crop failures due to unpredictable seasons affecting global supply chains.

6. Loss of Biodiversity

The Web of Life is Unraveling

Biodiversity conservation extends beyond the protection of charismatic species; it is fundamental to maintaining the stability of the Earth's life support systems and ecosystem services (IPBES, 2019).However, current biological trends indicate that we have entered the Sixth Mass Extinction event, with species loss rates occurring up to 100 times faster than the natural background rate (Barnosky et al., 2011; Ceballos et al., 2015).

Why it Matters: Ecosystem Services
  • Provisioning: Food, fresh water, pharmaceuticals (50% of drugs derived from nature).
  • Regulating: Climate regulation, flood control, water purification.
  • Supporting: Soil formation, nutrient cycling.

The "HIPPO" Drivers:

  • Habitat Destruction (Deforestation, Urbanization).
  • Invasive Species (Disrupting local chains).
  • Population Growth.
  • Pollution.
  • Overharvesting (Overfishing, Poaching).

IUCN Red List Status

Case Study: The Insect Apocalypse

40% of insect species are declining. Without pollinators like bees, 75% of global food crops are at risk.

7. Ocean Acidification

"The Other Carbon Problem"

While global warming dominates public discourse, the world's oceans have silently mitigated these effects by absorbing approximately 30% of all anthropogenic carbon dioxide (CO2) emissions (Feely et al., 2004; NOAA, 2020). This absorption effectively buffers the rate of atmospheric warming but fundamentally alters marine chemistry, leading to a phenomenon widely known as ocean acidification (Doney et al., 2009; IPCC, 2019).

The Chemical Reaction

$$ CO_2 + H_2O \rightarrow H_2CO_3 \rightarrow H^+ + HCO_3^- $$

The increase in Hydrogen ions ($H^+$) lowers the pH, making the water more acidic. This reduces the availability of Carbonate ions ($CO_3^{2-}$), which marine life needs to build shells.

At Risk: Pteropods (sea butterflies), Coral Reefs, Oysters, Clams, and Plankton.

Impact: Dissolving the Foundation

Healthy Ecosystem

pH 8.1 (Pre-industrial)

Collapsed Ecosystem

pH < 7.8 (Projected 2100)

Example: The Great Barrier Reef

Combined with warming waters causing bleaching, acidification weakens the coral skeleton, making recovery nearly impossible. 50% of the reef has been lost since 1995.

8. Genetic Engineering

Redefining Evolution

Genetic engineering involves the direct manipulation of an organism's genome using biotechnology tools such as CRISPR-Cas9 to alter DNA sequences (Doudna & Charpentier, 2014; National Human Genome Research Institute, 2024).While these technologies offer revolutionary solutions for medicine and agriculture, the release of Genetically Modified Organisms (GMOs) into unmanaged ecosystems poses significant risks, particularly regarding the potentially irreversible gene flow to wild relatives and non-target species (National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine, 2016; Snow et al., 2005).

The Promises

  • Reduced Chemical Use: Bt Cotton produces its own pesticide.
  • Climate Resilience: Drought-tolerant maize.
  • Bio-fortification: Golden Rice (Vitamin A) to fight malnutrition.

The Environmental Perils

  • Gene Flow: Pollen from GMOs contaminating wild relatives, creating "Superweeds".
  • Toxin Accumulation: Herbicide-resistant crops (Roundup Ready) encourage massive use of Glyphosate, killing soil biology.
  • Non-Target Harm: Bt toxins potentially harming beneficial insects like butterflies.

Ethical & Ecological Questions

"Terminator Seeds": Technology that renders seeds sterile after harvest, forcing farmers to buy new seeds annually, threatening food sovereignty.

Unintended Consequences: Once a modified organism is released into the biosphere, it cannot be "recalled".

1. Climate Change & GLOFs

The Melting "Third Pole"

Nepal, despite contributing only 0.027% to global greenhouse gas emissions, is the 4th most vulnerable country to climate change (Climate Risk Index). The Himalayas are warming at a rate significantly higher than the global average, leading to rapid glacial retreat.

GLOFs (Glacial Lake Outburst Floods)

Rapid melting creates unstable lakes dammed by loose moraine. Bursting leads to catastrophic floods downstream.
Example: Imja Tsho and Tsho Rolpa are critically dangerous.

Erratic Monsoons

Rainfall patterns are shifting—fewer rainy days but more intense downpours—leading to landslides in the hills and floods in the Terai, while disrupting agriculture.

Warming Rate: Himalayas vs Global

The Hindu Kush Himalaya (HKH) region is warming at approx 0.3°C to 0.7°C per decade, faster than the global average.

2. Air and Water Pollution (Nepal)

The Kathmandu Crisis

Kathmandu Valley often ranks among the most polluted cities globally due to its bowl-shaped topography which traps pollutants (Temperature Inversion).

Air Pollution Sources

  • Vehicular Emissions: Old vehicles, poor fuel quality.
  • Brick Kilns: Traditional coal-fired kilns in the valley outskirts.
  • Road Dust: Ongoing construction and unpaved roads.

Bagmati River Pollution

Once holy, the Bagmati is now biologically dead in urban stretches.

  • Direct Sewage: Dumping of untreated household waste.
  • Industrial Effluent: Carpets and garment factories dumping chemicals directly into the river.
  • Solid Waste: Riverbanks used as dumping sites.
PM2.5 Levels
Often exceeds 150 µg/m³ in winter (WHO safe limit is 5 µg/m³)

3. Deforestation & Land Degradation

"Hariyo Ban Nepal ko Dhan?"

Historically, Nepal's wealth was its green forests. However, rapid population growth and infrastructure development have led to significant forest cover loss, particularly in the Terai region.

Causes

  • Illegal logging and timber smuggling.
  • Encroachment for settlement and agriculture (Squatters).
  • Infrastructure: Roads, transmission lines, and hydropower.
  • Forest Fires: Increasing frequency during dry seasons.

Consequences: Land Degradation

  • Soil Erosion: Loss of topsoil in the hills reduces agricultural productivity.
  • Landslides: Deforested slopes become unstable during monsoons.
  • Desertification: Especially in the Chure range.

Forest Cover Status (approx 44%)

Community Forestry Success

Despite threats, Nepal's Community Forestry program is a global model for restoring hill forests, though Terai forests remain under severe pressure.

4. Illegal Mining of Sand & Stones

The "Dozer Terror"

The construction boom in Nepal and demand from neighboring India has fueled rampant, unregulated extraction of riverbed materials (sand, boulders, gravel). This is particularly devastating in the fragile Chure Range.

Riverbed Destruction

Lowers riverbeds, causing water tables to drop and drying up local wells and irrigation canals.

Infrastructure Damage

Exposure of bridge foundations leading to collapse (e.g., various bridges along the East-West Highway).

Chure Conservation Crisis

The Chure acts as a sponge for the Terai. Excavation here leads to flash floods and sedimentation downstream (desertification of Terai).

The Crusher Industry

"River Exploitation"

Policy Gap:

Despite bans on export and excavation in sensitive zones, weak enforcement and political protection allow the "Sand Mafia" to operate, altering natural river courses.

5. Poaching & Wildlife Trade

A Battle for Survival

Nepal is home to endangered species like the One-horned Rhino, Royal Bengal Tiger, and Snow Leopard. While conservation efforts have been laudable, poaching for body parts (used in traditional Chinese medicine) remains a persistent threat.

Targeted Species
  • One-horned Rhino: Poached for its horn.
  • Royal Bengal Tiger: Poached for skin and bones.
  • Red Panda: Poached for pelt and pet trade.
  • Pangolin: The most trafficked mammal in Nepal (scales).

Conservation Milestones

Nepal achieved Zero Poaching Years for Rhinos multiple times (2011, 2014, etc.) and was the first country to double its Tiger population (Tx2 Goal).

Rhino Population Growth

The population census shows a steady recovery due to strict army patrolling in National Parks.

6. Water Borne Diseases

Public Health Crisis

Poor sanitation infrastructure and the mixing of sewage with drinking water sources make water-borne diseases a leading cause of morbidity in Nepal, especially during the monsoon season.

Major Diseases

  • Cholera: Frequent outbreaks in Kathmandu and Terai.
  • Typhoid: Endemic across the country.
  • Dysentery/Diarrhea: Major cause of child mortality in rural areas.
  • Jaundice (Hepatitis E): Often linked to sewage contamination in water supply pipes.

Contamination

E. coli bacteria found in >70% of water sources in some districts.

ODF Status

Though declared Open Defecation Free (ODF), functional sanitation remains a challenge.

Fecal-Oral Route

The lack of separation between sewage lines and aging drinking water pipes (Melamchi distribution network facing leaks) creates a direct path for pathogens.
