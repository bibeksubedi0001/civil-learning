Chapter 8

Circular Motion

  • Motion of particle in circular path with constant or variable speed is called circular motion.

Uniform circular motion

  • Periodic motion in a circular path with constant speed.
  • Angular velocity: Angle swept by radius vector per sec.
    $W = \frac{2\pi}{T} = 2\pi f$
  • In uniform circular motions:
    1. Speed, angular speed, K.E, angular momentum remains constant.
    2. Velocity, linear momentum, force, acceleration & displacement get changed in direction only.
    3. Workdone $[\because \vec{f} \perp \vec{d}]$, angular acceleration $[\because \Delta W = 0]$ and moment of force about axis $[\because \vec{f} \perp \vec{d}]$ are zero.
    4. Force and acceleration are always $\perp$ to velocity and momentum.
    5. Tangential acceleration is zero and radial acceleration i.e., centripetal acceleration is.
      $a = \frac{v^2}{r} = \omega^2 r = 4\pi^2 f^2 r = \frac{4\pi^2}{T^2}r = v\omega$
[Diagram: Circle showing velocity $v$ tangential, and force $f$ / acceleration $a$ directed towards the center $O$]
  • $\omega$ perpendicular to the plane of circular motion i.e., along the axis.
  • Angle bet$^n$ velocity and acceleration is $90^\circ$
  • In uniform circular motion,
    Fc = Centripetal force
    $= \frac{Mv^2}{r} = m\omega^2r = \frac{(mr^2\omega)^2}{mr^2r} = \frac{L^2}{Ir}$
    L = angular momentum
    I = moment of inertia
  • Change in velocity $(\Delta V)$
    $= 2v \sin\frac{\theta}{2} = 2v \sin\frac{\omega t}{2}$
  • Change in displacement $(\Delta S)$
    $= 2R \sin\frac{\theta}{2} = 2R \sin\frac{\omega t}{2}$

Non-uniform circular motion

  • Motion of particle in a circular path with variable speed.
  • In non-uniform circular motion,
    • Velocity change in magnitude & direction both, so, particle has both tangential $\left[ \frac{dv}{dt} = r\alpha \right]$ and radial $\left[ \frac{v^2}{r} = \omega^2 r \right]$ acceleration thus, resultant acceleration a
      $a = \sqrt{a_t^2 + a_c^2}$
      where, $a_c = \frac{dw}{dt}$ (Note: Likely a typo in original text, should be related to radial accel)
    • Speed, K.E., momentum, angular momentum, angular velocity get changed.
    • Angular acceleration and work done are not zero.
    • Force and acceleration are not radially inward and are not perpendicular to velocity and momentum.
    • If velocity is increasing then angle between velocity and acceleration is acute and obtuse when velocity is decreasing.
    • Work done is not zero.
  • For a particle in motion if,
    1. $a_t = 0$ and $a_c = 0$, then particle is in uniform rectilinear motion.
    2. $a_t \neq 0$, then particle is in rectilinear motion with constant acceleration $a_t$.
    3. $a_t = 0$ and $a_c \neq 0$, then particle is in uniform circular motion with centripetal acceleration, $a = a_c$
    4. $a_t \neq 0$ and $a_c \neq 0$ then, particle is in variable circular motion with acceleration equal to
      $a = \sqrt{a_t^2 + a_c^2}$

Motion of a cyclist (Bicycle rider)

[Diagram: Cyclist leaning at an angle $\theta$ with the vertical. Reaction force $R$ resolved into $R\sin\theta$ (horizontal) and $R\cos\theta$ (vertical). Weight $mg$ acts downwards.]
  • If a bicycle rider turns without inclination, necessary centripetal force is provided by frictional force.
    $\frac{mv^2}{r} = \mu mg$
    $V = \sqrt{\mu rg}$
    This is max safe velocity for turning without inclination.
  • Condition for no skidding
    $\mu \ge \frac{v^2}{rg}$
  • If cyclist makes inclination $\theta$ with vertical, then centripetal force is provided by horizontal component of cyclist reaction.

Motion of vehicle on horizontal circular track

[Diagram: Rear view of a car on a horizontal track. Center of gravity at height $h$. Inner and outer wheel reactions $R_1$ and $R_2$.]

If $R_1$ and $R_2$ be reaction force of inner and outer wheel respectively.

$R_1 = \frac{mg}{2} \left(1 - \frac{v^2h}{gar}\right)$

$R_2 = \frac{mg}{2} \left(1 + \frac{v^2h}{gar}\right)$

$R_2 - R_1 = \frac{mv^2h}{ar}$

$R_1 + R_2 = mg$

Following conclusion can be drawn:

  1. when V = 0, then $R_1 = R_2 = \frac{mg}{2}$
  2. Reaction at inner wheel $R_1$ is less than reaction at outer wheel $R_2$
  3. When Speed V $\uparrow$es, then reaction at inner wheel $R_1 \downarrow$es and $R_2 \uparrow$es.
  4. When V = Critical velocity, $R_1$ = zero is inner wheels leaves the contact with ground and vehicle gets overturned outwardly.
    $V = \sqrt{\frac{gra}{h}}$ i.e., $V \alpha \sqrt{a} \; \alpha \sqrt{r} \; \alpha \frac{1}{\sqrt{h}}$
    h = height of c.g
    a = distance of a tyre form c.g

So,

  • Racing cars have low c.g.
  • Keeping luggage on the roof are prohibited because increased height of c.g, $\uparrow$es critical speed.
  • At sharp turning, vehicle gets overturned with low speed so roads are banked at turnings.

Motion of vehicle on banked circular track

[Diagram: Vehicle on a banked road with angle $\theta$. Reaction force $R$ resolved. $R\cos\theta$ upwards, $R\sin\theta$ towards center. Weight $mg$ downwards.]

When a vehicle moves an banked circular track of banking angle $\theta$, then

$R \cos\theta = Mg$ $R \sin\theta = \frac{Mv^2}{r}$

$\tan\theta = \frac{v^2}{rg} = \frac{H}{b} \quad \theta = \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{v^2}{rg}\right) = \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{H}{b}\right)$

The speed limit for certain banked tracks is

$V = \sqrt{gr\tan\theta} = \sqrt{\frac{grH}{b}}$

If speed of vehicle is less than this limiting speed, then vehicle slides radially outward for speed greater than $\sqrt{gr\tan\theta}$

Motion of body in vertical circle

[Diagram: Body of mass $m$ tied to a string moving in a vertical circle. Points A (bottom), B (horizontal), C (top), D (horizontal). Showing tensions and weight components.]

Tension at lowest position A,

$T_A = \frac{mv^2}{r} + mg = T_{max.}$

Tension at horizontal positions = $\frac{mv^2}{r}$

Tension at highest position C,

$T_C = \frac{mv^2}{r} - mg = T_{min.}$

Hence, speed, K.E, momentum, tension $\downarrow$es as body moves from lowest position to highest position.

  • Radial acceleration
    $a_r = \frac{v^2}{r} = \frac{T - mg\cos\theta}{m}$
  • Tangential acceleration,
    $a_t = \frac{F}{M} = \frac{mg\sin\theta}{m} = g\sin\theta$
  • Resultant acceleration, $a = \sqrt{a_t^2 + a_r^2}$
  • As body moves form lowest position to highest point, K.E. decrease P.E. increases but mechanical energy is conserved.
    So, decrease in K.E = increase in P.E.
    $\frac{1}{2}mv_A^2 - \frac{1}{2}mv_B^2 = mg \times 2r$
    $V_A^2 - V_B^2 = 4gr$
  • $T_{max} - T_{min} = T_A - T_B = 6mg$
  • At lowest point, critical velocity $V_A = \sqrt{5rg}$
  • At highest point, critical velocity $V_C = \sqrt{gr}$
    $V_A^2 - V_B^2 = 4gr$
  • Conditions for keeping the body in motion in vertical circle.
    1. Tension at highest position, $T_C \ge 0$
    2. Velocity at highest position, $V_C \ge \sqrt{gr}$
    3. Tension at lowest position, $T_A \ge 6mg$
    4. Velocity at lowest position, $V_A \ge \sqrt{5gr}$

If velocity ($V_A$) at lowest position is less than critical velocity i.e., ($<\sqrt{5rg}$) then two case occur.

  1. If $V_A \le \sqrt{2gr}$ then velocity becomes zero earlier than tension then body oscillates in vertical circle. In this case height ($h_1$) where velocity becomes zero is
    $h_1 = \frac{V_A^2}{2g}$
  2. If $\sqrt{2gr} < V_A < \sqrt{5gr}$ , then tension become zero earlier then velocity and body leaves the circular path and motion is ceased. In this case, height ($h_2$) where tension becomes zero is: $h_2=\frac{V_A^2 + gr}{3g}$

Motion of conical pendulum

[Diagram: Conical pendulum. String of length $l$ making angle $\theta$ with vertical. Tension $T$ resolved into $T\cos\theta$ and $T\sin\theta$. Radius $r$, height $h$.]

O $\rightarrow$ Centre of suspension

C $\rightarrow$ Centre of oscillation

$T\sin\theta = \frac{mv^2}{r}$
$T\cos\theta = mg$
$T = m\sqrt{g^2 + \frac{V^4}{r^2}}$
$l\sin\theta = R$
$h = l\cos\theta$

Now,

$\tan\theta = \frac{v^2}{gr} \leftarrow$ as $\theta \uparrow$es V $\uparrow$es.

$V = \sqrt{gr\tan\theta}$

$\omega = \frac{v}{r} = \sqrt{\frac{gr\tan\theta}{r}} = \sqrt{\frac{g\tan\theta}{r}} = \sqrt{\frac{g}{l\cos\theta}}$

$T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{r}{g\tan\theta}} = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{l\cos\theta}{g}} = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{h}{g}}$

  • For string to be completely horizontal $\theta = 90^\circ$ so speed should be infinite and time period should be zero. This explains that body can never be whirled in horizontal circle.
  • Angular speeds of angular velocities.
  • Angular velocities of:
    • Second hand $(\omega s) = \frac{2\pi}{60} \text{ rad s}^{-1} = \frac{\pi}{30} \text{ rad s}^{-1}$
    • Minute hand $(\omega m) = \frac{2\pi}{60} \text{ rad min}^{-1} = \frac{\pi}{1800} \text{ rad s}^{-1}$
    • Hour hand $(\omega n) = \frac{2\pi}{12} \text{ rad hr}^{-1} = \frac{\pi}{6 \times 3600} \text{ rad s}^{-1}$
    • Earth rotation $(\omega e) = \frac{2\pi}{24} \text{ rad hr}^{-1} = \frac{\pi}{12 \times 3600} \text{ rad s}^{-1} = \frac{\pi}{12} \text{ rad hr}^{-1}$

High Yield Points

  • Work done $[\vec{F} \perp \vec{d}]$, angular acceleration $[\because \Delta w = 0]$ and moment of force about axis $[\because \vec{f} \perp \vec{d}]$ are zero in uniform circular motion.
  • Tangential acceleration is zero and centripetal acceleration is $a = V^2/R = \omega^2 R = 4\pi^2 f^2 R = \frac{4\pi^2}{T^2} R = V\omega$ in uniform circular motion.
  • In uniform circular motion, change in velocity is $2V\sin\frac{\theta}{2}$ and change in displacement is $2R\sin\frac{\theta}{2}$.
  • $V = \sqrt{gr\tan\theta}$ is max speed.
  • Velocity at lowest position = $\sqrt{5rg}$
  • Velocity at highest position = $\sqrt{rg}$
  • Tension at lowest position = $\frac{mv^2}{r} + mg$
  • Tension at horizontal position = $\frac{mv^2}{r}$
  • Tension at highest position = $\frac{mv^2}{r} - mg$
  • Centrifugal force is an apparent force or pseudo force & is directed radially outward.
  • When a car driver takes a circular turn and his body is pressed against the side of car the force acting on him is centrifugal force.
  • $\frac{\omega_{hour}}{\omega_{earth}} = 2:1$, $\frac{\omega_{minute}}{\omega_{hour}} = 12:1$
  • A cyclist turns around curve at V Km/hr. If the turns at double of his speed, the tendency to overturn is quadrupled
    $\left[ \tan\theta = \frac{v^2}{gr} \right]$
  • Railway tracks are banked on curves for providing centripetal force from horizontal component of normal reaction on the track.
  • In a concave bridge, speed and force are max at pole.
  • Min rotational speed so that coin placed on a rotating table doesn't slip (i.e, move with table)
    $\omega_{min} = \sqrt{\frac{\mu g}{r}}$
    It is independent of mass of coin
    Hint: $F_{frictional} = F_{centripetal} = \mu mg = m\omega^2r$
  • Min rotational speed of a cylinder of radius r so as to enable on object of mass (m) remain stuck on the wall is
    $\omega_{min} = \sqrt{\frac{g}{\mu r}}$
  • If a body field to a string is in uniform circular motion, then, on releasing the string, body flies tangentially outward.
  • A body is moving with uniform velocity in circular path then centripetal acceleration is directed towards radius.
  • When a bucket filled with water is rotated in vertical circle, the water doesn't fall because centrifugal force balances the force of gravity.
  • Technometer = Measures speed of rotation.
  • If a particle at rest at highest position of a smooth vertical circle is slightly displaced, it will leave the circle at $h = \frac{2R}{3}$ where h is the height form center and R is radius of circle.
  • Angular velocity of a particle about any point of circumference of circle to center of circle is in ratio of 1:2
  • If 2 vehicles moving along 2 circular tracks of radius $r_1$ & $r_2$ at different constant sped ie, $V_1 \neq V_2$ complete revolutions in equal time $T_1 = T_2$ Then,
    $\omega_1 : \omega_2 = 1:1$
    $V_1 : V_2 = r_1\omega_1 : r_2\omega_2 = r_1 : r_2$
    $a_1 : a_2 = \omega_1^2r_1 : \omega_2^2r_2 = r_1 : r_2$
    If they move at constant speed i.e., ($V_1 = V_2$) then
    $V_1 : V_2 = 1:1$
    $\omega_1 : \omega_2 = r_2 : r_1$
    $a_1 : a_2 = r_2 : r_1$
  • When tube of length $l$ is filled completely with an incompressible fluid of mass m, closed at both ends and then rotated in horizontal plane with uniform angular velocity w, the force exerted on the other end by the liquid is $m\omega^2 \left[\frac{l}{2}\right]$
  • As a car move at constant speed on cover bridge from one end to and others end, the thrust of car on bridge first increases then decreases (as well as in concave road).
  • A body is revolving in circle at constant speed. If direction on its motion is reversed then centripetal force on body remains unchanged.
  • A bottle of soda water is revolved in vertical circle. The bubbles will be collected near neck.
  • When a vehicle moves on horizontal circular track then reaction of inner wheel is less than that of outer wheel.
  • When a body moves in uniform circular motion, no work is done on it.
